Parties

This agreement is between Josh Patterson and Museum LLC (together, "Josh") and the person who signs below (the "Recipient").

1. Purpose

Josh is sharing a business plan for DAD Record Cutters so the Recipient can decide whether to form a business partnership with Josh. The Recipient may use the Confidential Information only for that purpose.

2. Confidential Information

"Confidential Information" means everything in this presentation and anything Josh shares about it, in any form, including: the business plan and strategy; the pricing and cost model, financial projections and figures; the ownership, pay and profit-sharing structure; the DAD Record Cutters name, brand, logos, designs, artwork and mockups; product and research concepts; partnership concepts; and growth plans.

3. Ownership

The Confidential Information, and the ideas and concepts in it, belong to Josh Patterson and Museum LLC. Sharing it gives the Recipient no ownership or license, except the right to review it for the purpose in section 1.

4. No use without compensation

The Recipient will not use, develop, sell or disclose the Confidential Information, for the Recipient's own benefit or anyone else's, without a written agreement with Josh that includes compensation to Josh Patterson and Museum LLC. The Recipient will keep the Confidential Information private and will not share this presentation or its link.

5. What this agreement does not cover

The following belong to the Recipient or the public, and are not Confidential Information:

  1. Skills, knowledge and experience the Recipient had before receiving this presentation, including record cutting, mastering and machine building.
  2. The Recipient's own lathe designs, hardware, tools and cutting methods.
  3. Information that is already public, or becomes public without the Recipient breaking this agreement, such as published competitor prices and event fees.
  4. Information the Recipient develops independently without using the Confidential Information.
  5. Information the Recipient receives lawfully from someone else who is free to share it.

6. If we form the business

If Josh and the Recipient form DAD Record Cutters and sign an operating agreement, that operating agreement will say how the business-related Confidential Information is contributed or licensed to the company, and it will control over this agreement on that subject.

7. How long it lasts

The Recipient's obligations last two years from the date of signing. Any part of the Confidential Information that qualifies as a trade secret stays protected for as long as it remains a trade secret under Georgia law.

8. Returning information

On Josh's request, the Recipient will delete or return any copies, notes or files containing the Confidential Information.

9. No obligation

Neither party has to form a partnership or business by signing this agreement.

10. Governing law

This agreement is governed by the laws of the State of Georgia.

11. Electronic signature

Typing your full name, checking the box and selecting "Sign and open" is your electronic signature, with the same effect as signing by hand. A record of your name, email, the date and time, and this version is sent to Josh.

A2Product

Cut, not pressed. Finished, not blank.

Every record is cut in real time on lathes you built. The difference from the online shops is that a band gets the whole thing from us: mastering, the cut, printed labels and jackets designed in-house, and pickup in Athens.

7" single

A few minutes a side. The easy entry for a band's first record, and the strongest merch item.

10" EP

About 10–15 minutes a side. Our premium item, priced as one.

Runs of 10 to 50

Small runs for shows, tours and release nights. No 300-copy minimum like a pressing plant.

Athens Starter · $500. Ten 7" records, fully finished: mastering, the cut, designed labels and jackets, pickup in Athens. One flat price a band can say yes to. At $50 a record it's a release-night and collector item rather than merch-table stock.

Expansion

New equipment

We add lathes only when orders are waiting: bookings three weeks out, or Mike cutting more than 40 hours a month for two months running. Another double-lathe at Mike's build cost, about $896, pays back in roughly four more 10" runs. Mike builds unless a used commercial lathe costs less per hour of cutting. The dozen cutting-head templates get priced in R&D first.

Training Josh

Mike trains Josh in premastering, cutting and quality control, aiming for Josh to cut a full Athens Starter on his own by month six. Then a sick day or a busy month never stops production, and a second cutter comes before a second machine.

How we scale

In order: fill idle lathe time with gift records; outsource what doesn't need the lathe, like cassette duplication and jacket printing; hire a part-time packer at the floor rate once packing passes 10 hours a month; then add a cutter, then lathes. Josh's runway injections fund each step, and they're repaid as capital.

B3Ownership

60 / 40, with protections for both of us

Josh 60%
Mike 40%

I carry all the money and the risk, and I run everything outside the lathe. You bring the machines and the craft, and without you there's no record. 60/40 reflects both. Because we're both paid for our hours first, the split only governs the profit on top.

  • Vesting: ownership vests over 4 years, with a 1-year cliff.
  • Big decisions need us both: selling the business, taking on debt or adding an owner.
  • Buy-sell terms: if either of us wants out, the price and process are already agreed.
  • Quarterly ownership review: every quarter we look at logged hours and contributions and revisit the split together.
  • Quarterly runway injection: each quarter Josh adds cash to keep the business funded ahead of orders. It's logged as capital and paid back before profit is split.

An attorney will turn these into the operating agreement before any profit is paid.

B4Solve first

What we need to figure out together

  1. Cutting or embossing? Is your rig set up with a diamond cutting stylus? We're selling cut records, so this matters.
  2. Blank material. Can your lathes cut PVC blanks? They're about half the price of polycarbonate.
  3. Both lathes at once. Can one person run both at the same time? That's the biggest lever on your pay per hour.
  4. Your real numbers. Hours spent on the build, bad cuts per 25, and how long a stylus lasts.
  5. Cross-training. Mike teaches and trains Josh in record craftsmanship, so a sick day or a busy month never stops production.
The 10" problem. At about $43 a record, a band selling 10" records at $25 at the merch table loses money. We either sell the 10" as a premium item bands price at $35–40, or make a simpler version with band-supplied art and plain jackets. The 7" works better for merch.
